# Aven Cognitive Distortion Dataset Mapping & Annotation Notes

This document details the mapping decisions, dataset coverage gaps, and severity heuristic definitions used when training Aven's dual-head RoBERTa classifier (`AvenClassifier`) on the **Therapist Q&A Dataset (Shreevastava & Foltz, 2021)**.

---

## 1. Dataset Overview & Source

- **Primary Source**: Shreevastava & Foltz (2021), *Detecting Cognitive Distortions in Online Mental Health Forums / Therapist Q&A*.
- **Dataset Size**: ~2,530 real patient-therapist exchanges annotated with cognitive distortions.
- **Original Schema**: Single dominant label per example (+ optional secondary label) across 10 broad cognitive distortion categories.

---

## 2. Category Mapping Table (Shreevastava & Foltz 10 → Aven 15)

Aven's architecture tracks **15 distinct cognitive distortions** in `ml/model.py` (`DISTORTION_LABELS`). The table below details how the source dataset's 10 categories map to Aven's 15 targets:

| Shreevastava & Foltz (10 Categories) | Aven Target Category (`DISTORTION_LABELS`) | Mapping Rationale & Coverage Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| `All-or-nothing thinking` | `all_or_nothing` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Overgeneralization` | `overgeneralization` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Mental filter` | `mental_filtering` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Disqualifying the positive` | `disqualifying_positive` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Jumping to conclusions` | `jumping_to_conclusions` | Mapped to `jumping_to_conclusions`. Also used to weakly seed `mind_reading` & `fortune_telling` when specific sub-cues exist. |
| `Magnification (catastrophizing)` | `magnification`, `catastrophizing` | Source combines these. Examples with extreme negative outcome predictions are mapped to `catastrophizing`, while general exaggeration maps to `magnification`. |
| `Emotional reasoning` | `emotional_reasoning` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Should statements` | `should_statements` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Labeling and mislabeling` | `labeling` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |
| `Personalization` | `personalization` | Direct 1:1 match. Fully trained. |

### Untrained / Low-Coverage Categories in v1
The following Aven categories are **not covered by the Shreevastava & Foltz (2021) dataset**:
1. **`blame`**: Not present in the 10-category source schema. Marked as `0` in v1 training targets.
2. **`minimization`**: Source dataset groups magnification and catastrophizing together, lacking distinct minimization examples. Marked as `0` in v1 training targets.
3. **`mind_reading`**: Partially seeded from `jumping_to_conclusions` where text explicitly mentions interpersonal thoughts ("he thinks", "she knows"). Otherwise untrained in v1.
4. **`fortune_telling`**: Partially seeded from `jumping_to_conclusions` where temporal future markers ("going to fail", "will never work") are present. Otherwise untrained in v1.

> **Honest Disclosure for Paper / Benchmarks**: In v1, 11 out of 15 categories receive direct or seeded training data. Categories with 0 training samples (`blame`, `minimization`) will predict `0.0` confidence from the neural network and fall back to clinical rule-based heuristics if needed.

## 3. Severity Rating Method (Option B: Heuristic-Derived)

### Why Option B was Chosen
Aven's dual-head `AvenClassifier` architecture requires severity labels ($1 \le s \le 5$) per active distortion to compute `AvenClassifierLoss`. Because no public clinical dataset provides ground-truth 1–5 severity ratings per sentence, we implement **Option B**: a rule-governed linguistic heuristic.

> ⚠️ **DISCLAIMER**: Severity labels generated by this process are **heuristic-derived linguistic proxies**, NOT clinically validated diagnostic ratings. They serve to pre-train the model's severity head weights on emotional intensity cues.

### Severity Heuristic Algorithm (1 to 5 Scale)
For any text where a distortion is present:
- **Base Severity**: `2` (Moderate distortion present)
- **+1 Point**: Presence of extreme/absolutist modifiers (`always`, `never`, `impossible`, `completely`, `ruined`, `disaster`, `hate`, `horrible`, `every single`).
- **+1 Point**: High punctuation/formatting intensity (multiple exclamation marks `!`, ALL-CAPS words, or length > 120 words with high affective density).
- **-1 Point**: Presence of hedging/qualifying language (`maybe`, `a bit`, `sometimes`, `kind of`).
- **Final Clamp**: Severity is bounded to $[1, 5]$.
